Enhancing Wind Power Forecasting Accuracy in
Canada Using a Solar Data-Enhanced Hybrid Machine
Learning Model: Integrating ANN, LSTM, and SVR
Mahmoud M. Kiasari; Hamed H. Aly, SMIEEE
Electrical and Computer Engineering Department, Dalhousie University
Abstract— As the world moves toward sustainable energy
solutions, wind power emerges as a pivotal renewable energy
(RE) source due to its accessibility and zero carbon emission.
However, its unpredictable nature poses significant forecasting
challenges, that impact energy management efficiency. This
study tackles this vital challenge by integrating solar power data
into advanced machine learning models to enhance the forecast
accuracy and quantifying uncertainty of wind power. For this
work, the MERRA-2 dataset – a comprehensive atmospheric
reanalysis from NASA – spanning 2017 to 2019 across three
different locations of Canadian provinces, British Colombia,
Manitoba, and Nova Scotia has been considered. A novel hybrid
machine learning framework that combines the strengths of
Artificial Neural Networks (ANN), Long Short-Term Memory
Networks (LSTM), and Support Vector Machines (SVM), has been
announced. Utilizing this framework excels in pattern recognition,
temporal data processing, and regression analysis, effectively will
improve the precision of wind power forecasts. Besides, it provides
a robust framework for quantifying forecast uncertainty, thereby
enhancing decision-making in renewable energy management.
The superiority of this model is demonstrated through comparative
evaluations against conventional methods using various metrics to
establish its efficacy and applicability in real-world scenarios.
Keywords—Renewable Energy, Wind, Solar, Machine
Learning, MERRA 2
I. INTRODUCTION
Renewable energy sources have increasingly become vital
to global strategies in the quest for sustainable energy
solutions. Among all of them, wind power stands out due to its
considerable potential and environmental benefits. In recent
years, different countries have ramped up their investment in
wind power for the mitigation of impacts of climate change,
and carbon footprints, and reduced their reliability on fossil
fuels[1]. The International Energy Agency (IEA) highlights
that rapid technological advancements have not only reduced
the costs of renewable energy production but have also made
wind and solar energy increasingly competitive against
conventional fossil fuels. Despite all of the improvements, one
of the main challenges in harnessing wind power which is its
inherent intermittency still remains. Features like wind speed
and direction have an enormous impact on the energy
production. The unprecedented nature of wind features
emphasizes the necessity of using advanced forecasting and
management strategies to address grid integration issues,
investment risks, and operational efficiency.
Recent advancements in forecasting methodologies have
elevated the capabilities of ML algorithms to address the
unpredictability of wind generation. For instance, this study [2]
explores the use of ANN to predict wind speed and direction,
two important factors in wind generation the wind speed was
considered in this work, demonstrating a significant
improvement over traditional ones. Furthermore, the
integration of the LSTM network, highlighted by this work [3]
has offered promising results in capturing the temporal
dependencies in wind speed to refine the precision of wind
power prediction. The incorporation of SVM, into wind power
forecasting has also been studied in this paper [4], when
combined with other ML techniques, it can effectively model
the non-linearity between the wind speed and the power. The
role of environmental data, like solar irradiance, air
temperature, and precipitation, in enhancing the accuracy of
wind power forecasts has been increasingly acknowledged. A
thorough study [5] demonstrated how incorporating solar
power data into a wind forecasting model could signify the
precision in the accuracy of wind power by solar
interconnection. Alongside that, this paper [6] also proposed
different hybrid combinations of Adaptive Neuro – Fuzzy
Interference Systems (ANFIS ), Recurrent Kalman Filter (
RKF ), and Neuro-Wavelet ( WNN ), to demonstrate the
superior performance of hybrid models over the single method
ones.
The organization of this work is as follows. Section II
introduces each ML technique and its integration. Section III
is about data integration analysis and forecasting models. In
section IV, model implementation and validation have been
discussed, Results and future works are in sections V and VI,
respectively.

C. Model Configuration
In this section, intricate elaboration will be on the selected
ML models for the study. For this work, ANN, SVR, and
LSTMs have been chosen. The complexity of the data set, with
the varied variables and temporal dynamics, needed a profound
model can find the patterns among the features. For the ANN
and LTSM models, careful consideration was to their
hyperparameters, hidden layers or neurons within each layer,
to capture the correlation among the input datasets, thereby
enhancing predictive performance. The number of hidden
layers in the ANN model was designed in a way to commence
with a size roughly half of the input features, to employ a
geometric progression for decreasing the numbers of
subsequent layers. Using this way aids in gradually
concentrating the most useful information for the task.

Commonly used activation functions include Rectified Linear
Units (ReLU), sigmoid, and SoftMax, each of them offering
unique features that would be used in different types of
modeling scenarios[14].

III. INTEGRATING ANN, SVR, AND LTSM FOR PREDICTIVE
MODELING IN WIND POWER FORECASTING
Forecasting has faced an enormous advance with the
advent of ML. This transformation mainly relies on the vast
amount of data that could be gathered by industries that are
collected in datasets or websites like “Renewable Ninja”,
which researchers and engineers have access to[16]. Among
all ML techniques, this work focused on three specific ones.
ANN, SVR ( the regression model of SVM ), and LSTM were
chosen for their ability to handle the non-linear and intricate
patterns characteristic of wind data[2]. Having these three
models as a hybrid ML framework offers a promising approach
for optimal RE forecasting.
• ANN: Utilizing the pattern recognition capabilities
inherent in ANN, inspired by the neural architectures of
animal brains, this model excels in identifying intricate
patterns and dependencies within complex, dynamic
datasets, such as those encountered in wind power time
series[17].
• SVR: Chosen for its proficiency in managing high-
dimensional data, SVR applies the kernel trick to
effectively tackle the non-linear relationships found in
wind power data, optimizing the balance between model
complexity and predictive accuracy essential in volatile
environments [5].
• LSTM: As a specialized form of Recurrent Neural
Networks (RNNs), LSTM is particularly adept at capturing
long-term dependencies in time series data, a critical
feature in the predictability of wind patterns [18].
IV. MODEL IMPLEMENTATION AND VALIDATION
A. Data Preparation and Preprocessing
The foundation of this model implementation began with
the data collection phase, where MERRA-2 was used , which
is acclaimed for its comprehensive atmospheric reanalysis
capabilities. The spatial resolution of the MERRA-2 data is
approximately 0.5° x 0.625° (latitude x longitude), which
equates to about 50km x 50km at the equator, providing
detailed geographical coverage sufficient to capture diverse
climatic conditions across Manitoba, Nova Scotia, and British
Columbia spinning the years 2017 to 2019. One hour time
interval for 1095 days from the MERRA-2 as inputs for
network training, 10 × 236314 numbers of dataset with their
within features, and wind generation taken as the target data.
They have divided into 70% for training, 15% for validation,
and the rest for testing. The data characterized included metrics
from three different turbines: Enercon E126 6500, Siemens
SWT 3.6, and V90 2000. The specifics of steps and
input/output data can be seen in Fig. 4 and Fig. 5.
B. Model Architecture and Hybrid Model Formulation
The work offered the integration of two sophisticated
neural networks: the ANN and LSTM. The ANN component
was designed with an input layer of 256 neurons, followed by
two hidden layers each comprising 128 neurons, all in ReLU
activation function, and culminating in a linear activation
function as an output layer. The aim was this structure could
capture the complex relationship among the features.
Parallelly, the LSTM model has 128 neurons as its first layer,
followed by 64 neurons at another one, all in ReLU, and lastly
finished by one linear activation function hidden layer. The
usage of this model helped to modify the deep temporal
dependencies among features. A novel aspect of this work was
the integration of the last two mentioned models, serving as a

VI. FUTURE WORKS
The proposed Hybrid model, in the field of R2 and MSLE,
achieved better results than other methods. This improvement
suggests that this model could provide more reliable
predictions, potentially reducing the operational risks and cost
in the wind power management. However, it is important to
note that, the current model has been tested under conditions
specific to the Canadian provinces of British Colombia,
Manitoba, and Nova Scotia. Based on the success of the hybrid
model, future works could benefit from exploring several
avenues for enhancement and refinement. One potential area
of focus could be the integration of additional environmental
factors, such as atmospheric pressure, to expose its beneficial
impact on the model performance and accuracy. Moreover,
considering different locations or various turbines could
provide valuable insights into the generalizability and
applicability of the hybrid model in diverse situations. Last but
not least, combining other ML methods, exclusively deep
learning ones, could aid the model in obtaining a better
understanding of the dataset for further accurate and
wholesome predictions.
