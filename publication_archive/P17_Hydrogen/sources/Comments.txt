Comments to the Author
The paper proposes a multi-agent reinforcement learning approach to extend electrolyzer life in offshore wind-hydrogen systems. The paper presents a modular decision pipeline combining an LSTM forecaster, a soft actor-critic (SAC) controller, and a quantile regression deep Q-network (QR-DQN) arbitrage module. SAC and QR-DQN are two components that use reinforcement learning (RL). Detailed comments are given below.

1. The formulation does not yet satisfy a multi-agent RL because Agent 1 is a supervised LSTM forecaster, not an RL agent; it has no actions, reward, policy, or environment interaction. Agent 2 and Agent 3 are RL modules, but they are trained sequentially with fixed upstream agents, which makes the framework closer to a hybrid hierarchical control pipeline: supervised forecasting + SAC control + QR-DQN arbitrage.
2. The introduction is long and not referenced accordingly. The authors provide quantitative results of prior studies without providing the corresponding references.
3. The authors stack multiple references at the end of the paragraph, but references need to be distributed accordingly.
4. The authors need to define how probabilistic forecasts are produced and calibrated, and show their results along with statistical significance.
5. The authors must separate baseline design life, simulated volatile-wind life, and economic stack-life metric. The abstract claims an 8.1% extension of electrolyzer stack life, but the conclusion claims a 21.3% electrolyzer life extension.
6. Table 2 should be referenced.
7. Table 3 shows a curtailment utilization of 78.2%. However, section 1.b mentions a curtailment utilization of 30.6%.
8. The manuscript should be revised to fix all inconsistencies throughout the manuscript and provide one final result.
9. The paper states results are reported as mean +-95% confidence interval, but Table 3 and the figures report single values only.
10. Reference [29] is cited to support a PEM electrolyzer thermal degradation threshold, but the listed paper is about Pd–Ag catalysts for ethanol electrooxidation.
11. Several references are duplicated or inconsistent: [1] and [28] appear to be the same paper; [4] and [26] are the same Rakousky paper; [5] and [21] are the same Scheepers paper.
12. The manuscript needs a thorough proofreading to fix the typos and inconsistencies. The current manuscript reads like a report with multiple bullet points and small paragraphs containing one sentence.
